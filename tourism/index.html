Where we run

Six regions, each with a lead guide who lives in it. Nobody flies in to run a route they only know from the map.

Why the groups are small

Fourteen people is the point at which a village stops being able to feed you.

Above that number a trip needs its own logistics: coaches, hotels booked in blocks, kitchens that cook for groups rather than for guests. Below it you can stay in a guesthouse with nine rooms, eat what the family eats, and change the plan on the morning the weather turns.

So fourteen is our ceiling, and most routes run well under it. On the Ausangate circuit it is eight, because that is how many horses one arriero can manage over a 5,000-metre pass.

“They moved the whole itinerary a day early because the forecast turned. We crossed the pass in sunshine and heard afterwards that the group behind us didn't get over at all.”
— Alta Via 1, September 2025
What we commit to

Three things, in writing

Guides are local and paid year-round

Every route has a lead guide from the region, on a retainer through the off-season. Guiding here is a job, not a summer.

Prices include what you'd otherwise be asked for

Park fees, permits, transfers and hut bookings are in the price. What isn't included is listed on every route page, with amounts.

We cancel rather than push on

If conditions are wrong we reroute or refund. No trip has ever gone ahead because it was already paid for.
